Drafting with a model saves the blank page. Sending with a model creates the wrong recipient, the extra promise, and the internal note in the customer’s inbox. The send button stays with a person.

## Four kinds of mail

| Kind | Model writes | You still do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Receipt | One line, no date promise | Confirm there is no commitment |
| Request for info | Questions and a short reply | Check attachments and times |
| Push the work forward | Next step and open points | Delete promises you cannot keep |
| Sensitive | A summary of risks only | Write the mail yourself |

Complaints, quotes, contracts, and HR mail are sensitive. A polished apology that offers a refund is worse than a slow, accurate reply.

## The draft shape

Ask for two blocks. The customer block may not claim an attachment you did not name, and it must mark unknown dates or amounts as `[to fill]`. The internal block lists what a reader might think you promised. You paste only the first block.

Before sending, check: recipients and cc, attachments that the text mentions, no leftover `[to fill]`, no promise you cannot keep, tone that matches the relationship, and no internal sentence in the body.

If you delete most drafts of one kind, stop asking for a sendable draft. Ask for a summary instead. The prompt skeleton lives in [A prompt template you can reuse](/en/posts/prompt-engineering-templates/).
