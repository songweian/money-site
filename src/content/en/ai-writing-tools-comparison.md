Writing tools look alike on a pricing page. The difference shows up on a paragraph you actually need to send.

## Three samples, not a demo

Run the same three pieces on each candidate:

1. A short rewrite, such as a customer email, with a word limit and a tone.
2. A long document you wrote, asking for a summary that must keep the exceptions in the second half.
3. A question about a current install command or price. Then open the official page. If the tool invented a flag, stop.

| Result | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Format ignored | The tool is a poor fit, or your output rules are too soft |
| Second half missing | The context window, or the way you pasted, dropped the tail |
| Stale command, said with confidence | Do not use it for anything that changes monthly |

## Free plans

Quota, context length, browsing, and whether chats are used to train a product all move. Check the policy the day you start, especially for client copy. One account per job is enough. A pile of free logins scatters the prompts you will want next week.

When a plan runs out every Wednesday, pay for that one job or switch. Do not open another inbox to dodge the limit. Broader selection advice is in [Choose AI tools that save time](/en/posts/best-ai-productivity-tools/).
