A long list of AI products does not tell you what to install on Monday. Start from the hour you lose every week, then pick one tool for that hour.

## Sort the work before you sort the tools

Spend a day noting where time goes. Four buckets are enough: writing, coding, repeated chores, and reading unfamiliar material. The bucket that shows up three times a week is the one to tool first. A rare, messy task can wait.

## What actually differs

| Job | What to compare | What to ignore at first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Writing | Whether it keeps your format and tone | Leaderboard scores |
| Coding | Whether private code is allowed to leave the company | Feature checklists |
| Automation | Whether the steps are stable enough to script | A model that "might" click a UI |
| Research | Whether answers cite a source you can open | Confident prose with no link |

Free limits, context length, and data-use terms change often. Read them on the day you sign up. If a clause is unclear, do not paste customer files or unreleased code.

## A two-week trial

Days 1–2: pick three real tasks from last week. Days 3–5: one tool per task, using work you already have, not demo prompts. Days 6–8: save the prompts that worked. Days 9–10: automate one chore you repeat. Days 11–14: drop anything you did not open again.

The only score that matters is whether setup time was smaller than time saved. If it was not, that job is not ready.
