People do not fail at note-taking because they chose the wrong app. They fail because every article lands in a different place, and nothing is rewritten in their own words.

## One inbox

Pick a single capture point: one inbox note, one read-later app, or the share sheet into one folder. Browser bookmarks, chat stars, and three cloud drives are three systems. You will not search all of them.

Once a week, empty the inbox. Each item becomes a project note, a reference you might cite, or trash. If you cannot say which, it is trash.

## A small map

| Place | Holds | Does not hold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inbox | Unsorted captures from this week | Finished writing |
| Projects | Notes tied to something with a deadline | "Someday" articles |
| Reference | Facts you looked up twice | Your opinions mixed in unlabeled |
| Archive | Done projects | The live to-do list |

PARA-style folders and "one folder per project" both work. Do not run both. The test is whether a teammate can find last month's decision in under a minute.

## Rewrite, then stop

A highlight is not a note. A note says what you will do with the idea, in a sentence you wrote. Models can summarize a PDF into that sentence, but you still delete the lines you do not believe. Store the summary next to the source link so you can check it.

Tool choice is secondary. If the team already lives in one docs product, use that. Switching apps is the usual way a second brain dies. For how a small team should limit tools, see [A toolchain for teams of 3 to 10](/en/posts/small-team-toolchain-setup/).
